Designing a home for heat and heavy rain

Practical choices for roofs, ventilation and drainage that keep a house cooler and drier year-round.

[Publish date]   By [Author]

A house in a hot climate with intense seasonal rain has two jobs: keep heat out and move water away quickly. Good decisions at the design stage cost little and make a big difference to comfort and maintenance.

Generous roof overhangs

Deep eaves shade walls and windows from the sun and protect them from driving rain, which reduces both heat gain and damp problems.

Cross ventilation

Place openings on opposite sides of rooms so air can move through the house. High-level vents let hot air escape.

Roof and wall insulation

A roof absorbs a lot of heat. Insulation or a ventilated ceiling space under the roof sheet reduces the heat that reaches the rooms below.

Drainage planned from day one

Plan gutters, downpipes and surface drains with the site levels so storm water flows away from the foundations. Raising the floor level above the surrounding ground helps keep water out.

Durable finishes

Choose finishes that tolerate humidity: good waterproofing in wet areas, quality external paint, and tiles or screeds that are easy to clean.
